## Recommended narrative paragraph (drop-in ready)

Specialty medications — biologics and other complex, high-cost therapies typically priced above CAD $10,000 for a one-year course of treatment — account for a disproportionate and rising share of public and private drug spending in Canada [Grundy et al. 2023]. In response, pharmaceutical manufacturers have built an extensive infrastructure of manufacturer-sponsored patient support programs (PSPs): a recent cross-sectional mapping of all 2,556 prescription drugs marketed in Canada found that roughly 10% had an accompanying PSP, with programs strongly concentrated among brand-name, biologic, orphan, and high-cost products (drugs priced at more than $100 per unit were over 10 times more likely to have a PSP than those priced $1–$10 per unit) [Grundy et al. 2023]. These PSPs — typically delivered by third-party specialty administrators — bundle reimbursement navigation (≈90% of programs), nurse-led clinical case management (≈87%), injection/infusion training, home delivery, and patient education, and increasingly serve as a real-world data infrastructure surrounding specialty therapies [Grundy et al. 2023; Wills et al. 2023]. This institutional context motivates Canada-specific attention to how information about specialty medications is represented and retrieved to support patient-facing question answering.

## Verified citations

### [1] Grundy et al. 2023 — primary citation (replaces the misattributed "Quaye et al." candidate)
- **Full citation:** Grundy Q, Quanbury A, Hart D, Chaudhry S, Tavangar F, Lexchin J, Gagnon M-A, Tadrous M. Prevalence and nature of manufacturer-sponsored patient support programs for prescription drugs in Canada: a cross-sectional study. *CMAJ* 2023;195(46):E1565–E1576.
- **DOI:** 10.1503/cmaj.230841 · **PMID:** 38011930 · **PMCID:** PMC10681678
- **Open access:** Yes, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Full PDF at https://www.cmaj.ca/content/cmaj/195/46/E1565.full.pdf and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10681678/
- **Why it's useful:** The most comprehensive peer-reviewed mapping of Canadian manufacturer-sponsored PSPs; explicitly defines specialty medicines as those "typically priced at more than $10,000 for a 1-year course of treatment," quantifies PSP prevalence (10.0% of 2,556 marketed drugs; 61.7% of 89 manufacturers offer ≥1 PSP), and demonstrates statistically that PSPs are concentrated among brand-name (77.7%), biologic (53.9%), orphan (46.1%), and high-unit-cost products. Single citation covering both the "specialty-drug prevalence/cost" and "institutional PSP mapping" framing points.

### [2] Wills, Mitha & Cheung 2023 — secondary citation (PSP as data/RWE infrastructure)
- **Full citation:** Wills A, Mitha A, Cheung WY. Data collection within patient support programs in Canada and implications for real-world evidence generation: the authors' perspective. *Journal of Pharmaceutical and Pharmaceutical Sciences* 2023;26:11877.
- **DOI:** 10.3389/jpps.2023.11877 · **PMID:** 37901362 · **PMCID:** PMC10603246
- **Open access:** Yes, full text at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10603246/
- **Why it's useful:** Establishes that "PSP infrastructure [is] already in place for most specialized drugs in Canada" and positions PSPs as a real-world-evidence data source surrounding specialty medications. Supports the framing that PSPs function as information/data infrastructure around specialty drugs.
- **Caveat:** Two of three authors (Wills, Mitha) are affiliated with 20Sense Corp., a specialty-medicines industry consultancy; cite as a perspective piece rather than as independent empirical evidence. Cheung is the academic co-author (U. Calgary).
