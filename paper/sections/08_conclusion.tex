\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented the first paired-data comparison of narrative RAG, naive structured FHIR
RAG, and resource-aware structured FHIR RAG on specialty-medication adherence-indicator
question answering, where information content is held constant across representations
by shared-source rendering from a single FHIR bundle per patient.
Narrative RAG achieves the highest exact-match accuracy (40.6\% vs.\ 33.4--35.3\%).
A 1024-token sensitivity sweep on a stratified 495-question sample shows the
structured systems gain $+3.8$ to $+5.1$~pp when the answer budget is doubled while
the narrative system is unchanged, narrowing but not eliminating A's lead; the
A $>$ B $>$ C ordering is therefore not a token-budget artefact alone.
A templated-narrative ablation that uses no LLM
(\S\ref{sec:results:templated}) matches the LLM-narrative arm overall
(41.3\% vs.\ 40.6\%, $\Delta{=}{+}0.74$~pp [$+0.13$, $+1.34$]), so the
narrative-format advantage is not specific to LLM-generated text.  A
no-retrieval baseline (\S\ref{sec:results:noretrieval}) decomposes each
system's accuracy into a $25.2\%$ question-text-attributable floor plus
retrieval-attributable lifts of $+15.4$ (A), $+10.1$ (B), and $+8.2$ (C)~pp,
all with CIs excluding zero.
Temporal-anchor failures are a structural weakness of the narrative format that
persists independent of budget.  In the feature-extraction arm, structured FHIR
features substantially outperform both narrative-derived and retrieval-trace features
for adherence classification, on a synthetic label whose alignment with the
structured representation is acknowledged and discussed (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
The pattern -- narrative format wins on QA, structured features encode the
adherence signal more directly under the synthetic label -- is consistent with
a \emph{representation dissociation}: winning natural-language QA and winning
downstream adherence-outcome ML are different objectives on this cohort, and
the same representation does not simultaneously optimise both.

Code (three retrieval systems, evaluation harness, feature extractors), analysis
scripts, and the synthetic dataset (200 patients, 13,800 questions, five PSP
adherence-indicator families) are released as a combined Zenodo deposit at
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20263384}{doi:10.5281/zenodo.20263384}
under Apache-2.0 (code) and CC-BY-4.0 (dataset), providing a reusable benchmark
for the specialty-pharmacy QA research community.

Future directions include: (1) extending the budget sweep to additional points
(256, 768, 2048) and to the full 13,800-question matrix; (2) evaluating on real
de-identified PSP data; (3) testing robustness across multiple answer LLMs and
embedding models; (4) extending the question bank to additional specialty-medication
classes and real-world edge cases (infusion regimens, variable-interval dosing,
bridge therapies); (5) constructing richer synthetic adherence labels to stress-test
the feature-extraction arm beyond the perfectly-scheduled synthetic scenario;
(6) a drop-one ablation over System~C's three resource-aware primitives (typed filter,
reference-chain traversal, temporal pre-filter) to disentangle which primitive drives
the recall@$k$ gain over System~B; and (7) constructing hybrid narratives that select
deterministic table-like phrasing for date-arithmetic question families (where the
templated arm beats the LLM arm by $+10.21$~pp on \texttt{temporal\_comparison}) and
LLM prose for dose-aggregation families (where the LLM arm beats the templated arm
by $+7.29$~pp on \texttt{regimen\_aggregation}), with the per-family-optimal hybrid
expected to beat both single-style narratives on a workload mix by construction.
