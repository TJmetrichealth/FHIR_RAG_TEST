\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We compared narrative RAG, naive structured FHIR RAG, and resource-aware structured FHIR
RAG on 13,800 programmatically verified adherence-indicator questions over paired
renderings of 200 synthetic specialty-regimen patients.  On aggregate exact-match the
narrative arm leads (40.6\% vs.\ 35.3\% and 33.4\%), and the lead survives a
patient-level cluster bootstrap.  Taking that lead apart shows what it is made of.  Under
the pre-specified scorer it is entirely abstention: the narrative states which components
a regimen lacks and the structured record does not, so the narrative arm answers N/A
correctly far more often, while on the 8,177 questions with a substantive answer all
three arms score 15--16\% and fall below a constant per-family guess.  A predictor that
always answers N/A matches the narrative arm overall, and the previously reported Tier-3
collapse is the disappearance of N/A questions at Tier~3.  Scoring only the final answer
instead of the whole reasoning trace raises the narrative arm's substantive accuracy to
28.7\% and leaves the structured arms near 17\%, but the residual lead sits in exactly
the families whose answers the narrative generation prompt writes into the text and is
absent where both representations state the answer equally.  We conclude that the
advantage is a product of explicit absence statements, summary leakage and scorer
behaviour, not of prose as a format.

We also disclose three defects found by auditing our own released outputs: the question
bank's reference date was never passed to the model, the answer LLM ran in thinking mode
under a 512-token cap and was truncated in roughly a third of responses, and the scorer
reads the reasoning trace and mis-scores categorical free-text answers.  We have
quantified each from the frozen data and stated which earlier claims they invalidate,
rather than reissuing the aggregate result.  The feature-extraction arm is retained as an
exploratory supplement and no longer supports a representation-dissociation claim.

The constructive findings are these.  Structured-record RAG needs an explicit mechanism
for stating absence.  Benchmarks in which N/A is a valid answer must report N/A prevalence
per stratum and accuracy on N/A and substantive questions separately, alongside a
constant-N/A baseline.  Any summary generated upstream of retrieval should be audited
against the question bank, because what it pre-computes will be credited to the retrieval
pipeline.  A deterministic templated narrative is at least as good a narrative source as
an LLM for regimen summaries of this kind.  And windowed adherence counts and ratios
should be computed, not asked of a language model.

Code, analysis scripts, and the synthetic dataset are released as a combined Zenodo
deposit at \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20292792}{doi:10.5281/zenodo.20292792}
under Apache-2.0 (code) and CC-BY-4.0 (dataset).  The required next steps, in order, are:
(1)~interpolate the reference date into every question string; (2)~rerun all arms with
thinking disabled or a multi-thousand-token budget, stating which; (3)~correct the scorer
to parse only the final answer and to match categorical ground-truth tokens; (4)~add an
overlay-only structured arm, a compact structured arm, and a narrative arm with
aggregates and listed dates removed; (5)~add a tool-calling arm as the reference ceiling;
(6)~run the drop-one ablation over System~C's primitives; and (7)~report every result
stratified by N/A status with cluster-robust intervals.  Only after these steps can the
substrate question this paper set out to answer be answered.
