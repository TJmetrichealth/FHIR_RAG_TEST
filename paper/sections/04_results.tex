\label{sec:results:overall}

Taken at face value, Tables~\ref{tab:main_results} and~\ref{tab:pairwise} reproduce the

\paragraph{The narrative lead is an abstention effect.}
On the 5,623 N/A questions the narrative arms are far better at answering N/A: A~76.4\%
and A-T~79.9\% against B~64.7\% and C~59.2\%.  On the 8,177 questions with a substantive
answer, the three primary systems score 16.0\%, 15.1\% and 15.6\%.  The cluster-bootstrap
differences on the substantive subset are A vs.\ B $+0.86$~pp [$+0.37$, $+1.34$],
A vs.\ C $+0.35$~pp [$-0.11$, $+0.82$], and B vs.\ C $-0.50$~pp [$-0.91$, $-0.09$]: the
A vs.\ C gap is not significant, and the B vs.\ C gap reverses in favour of C.  The
templated arm, which leads A in aggregate, trails it on substantive questions
($-1.19$~pp [$-1.61$, $-0.75$]).  Under the pre-specified scorer, every ordering claimed
in the previous version of this paper depends on the N/A questions.  The sensitivity
analysis in \S\ref{sec:results:flaws} shows that the substantive-question tie is itself
partly a scorer artefact; the abstention finding is not.

The mechanism is visible in the narratives themselves.  Both narrative generators state
what the regimen consists of (``This regimen consists of one medication component,
referred to as the primary component''), so a question about an oral adjunct that does not
exist is answered N/A from an explicit negative statement.  The serialised FHIR bundle
contains no statement of absence: a question about a non-existent component retrieves the
nearest existing MedicationAdministration resources, and the model computes a value for
the wrong component.  System~C's typed router makes this worse, because it restricts the
candidate pool to medication resources and so guarantees that the context is dense with
records of the component that does exist (Tier~1 N/A accuracy: B~71.7\%, C~60.4\%).

\paragraph{The complexity gradient is a label-distribution effect.}
Aggregate accuracy falls from 41--54\% at Tiers~1--2 to about 20\% at Tier~3 for every
system (Table~\ref{tab:tier_results}; Figure~\ref{fig:accuracy_by_tier}).  Within the
substantive subset the decline is much smaller (A: 18.2\% $\rightarrow$ 19.3\%
$\rightarrow$ 13.6\%), and within the N/A subset accuracy at Tier~3 is only modestly
below Tiers~1--2.  What changes across tiers is the share of N/A ground truth, from
54--59\% to 13.8\%.  The Tier-3 ``collapse'' reported previously is therefore mostly
the disappearance of easy N/A questions, not a qualitative failure on multi-drug regimens.

\paragraph{Every system is below a trivial baseline on substantive questions.}
The per-family most-frequent-answer baseline, computed within the substantive subset,
scores 28.1\%, 21.8\% and 28.7\% at Tiers~1--3, above every system at every tier
(final column of Table~\ref{tab:na_split}).  In the previous version we reported this
comparison at Tier~3 only, with a baseline of 24.8\% that did not admit N/A as a
candidate label; with N/A admitted the Tier-3 baseline is 29.5\% over all Tier-3
questions.  Under the pre-specified scorer, no arm beats a constant per-family guess on
questions that have an answer; under final-answer scoring (\S\ref{sec:results:flaws})
System~A reaches the baseline and the structured arms remain well below it.

\subsection{Three Pipeline Defects and Their Reach}
\label{sec:results:flaws}

The post-review audit of the frozen raw outputs surfaced three defects.  We quantify each
from the released data.

\paragraph{Defect 1: the reference date was never supplied.}
7,800 canonical question strings refer to ``the reference date'', but the date lives in a
metadata field that no system received (\S\ref{sec:methods:dataset}).  The model can
succeed on such a question only by answering N/A, or by guessing an anchor from dates in
the context.  This explains the near-zero substantive accuracy on
\texttt{regimen\_compliance} (windowed counts and ratios relative to the reference date)
and on the \texttt{tc.ref\_vs\_last} templates (``before, on, or after the reference
date''), where the final answers are predominantly N/A.  It also removes the basis for the
previous version's claim that temporal-anchor failures are a structural weakness of the
narrative format: the anchor was missing for every arm, and the structured arms merely had
more dates in context from which to guess.  The differential temporal-anchor rates in the
earlier 50-failure taxonomy (15 vs.\ 9 vs.\ 5 of 50) are, in any case, not significant
between A and B (Fisher exact $p=0.24$).

\paragraph{Defect 2: thinking mode under a 512-token cap.}
All 69,000 responses begin a \texttt{<think>} block.  Table~\ref{tab:budget_audit}
reports, from the billed token counts, how often the budget was exhausted before the
block closed.  Between 30.6\% and 38.2\% of retrieval-arm responses are truncated inside
the reasoning trace and never state a final answer; the no-retrieval arm, which has
nothing to reason over, is truncated 1.4\% of the time.  Truncation is higher for the
structured arms at Tiers~1--2 and higher for the narrative arm at Tier~3.  The
1024-token sweep (\S\ref{sec:results:budget_sweep}) shows that doubling the budget leaves
12--16\% of responses still truncated.

\paragraph{Defect 3: the scorer reads the reasoning trace and mis-scores categorical
free text.}
For date and numeric ground truths the scorer takes the first ISO date or first number
anywhere in the response, including inside the \texttt{<think>} block.  A truncated
response can therefore be scored correct if the right value happens to appear early in
the reasoning, and a completed response can be scored wrong if a different value appears
first.  For categorical free-text ground truths (component names and comparators) the
scorer checks for bundle-derived entity strings rather than the ground-truth token, and
this path credits no correct answers at all: substantive \texttt{temporal\_comparison}
accuracy is 0.0\% for every arm, although the ground-truth token appears in the final
answer of 23--26\% of those responses.  The same path depresses substantive
\texttt{cross\_resource} accuracy (the ground-truth token appears in the final answer of
about 70\% of responses, against 34--43\% scored correct).  We therefore treat the
substantive \texttt{temporal\_comparison} row as unmeasured and the substantive
\texttt{cross\_resource} rows as lower bounds.

\paragraph{Sensitivity: scoring only the final answer.}
To bound the effect of Defect~3 we re-scored every arm on the text after the closing
\texttt{</think>} tag only, treating the responses that never closed the block as empty
(\path{analysis/run_postthink_rescore.py}; no model calls).  Table~\ref{tab:postthink}
compares the two scorings.  The change is large and asymmetric.  System~A's substantive
accuracy rises from 16.0\% to 28.7\% (1,229 questions move from wrong to right and 665
the other way), whereas B and C move by about two points (16.8\% and 17.7\%).  Under
final-answer scoring the narrative arm therefore leads the structured arms on
substantive questions by 11--12~pp (cluster CIs: A vs.\ B $+11.86$ [$+11.21$, $+12.53$];
A vs.\ C $+10.98$ [$+10.34$, $+11.65$]), matches the per-family modal baseline at
Tier~1 (28.1\% vs.\ 28.1\%), exceeds it at Tier~2 (33.1\% vs.\ 21.8\%) and falls just
below it at Tier~3 (27.1\% vs.\ 28.7\%); B and C stay below the baseline at every tier
under both scorings.  N/A accuracy falls for every arm because truncated responses no
longer receive credit for a stray N/A in the reasoning.

Where does the final-answer substantive lead come from?  Table~\ref{tab:postthink_family}
breaks it down by family.  On \texttt{cross\_resource} (component presence, schedule
kind, tier), which both representations state explicitly, the three arms are tied
(39.5\%, 38.9\%, 40.6\%).  The lead is concentrated on \texttt{temporal\_lookup}
(44.6\% vs.\ 27.5\% and 27.8\%), where most substantive templates ask for the first, last,
or next administration date or the regimen start and end, all of which the generation
prompt instructs the narrative to list verbatim; on \texttt{regimen\_aggregation}
(34.6\% vs.\ 17.9\% and 18.9\%), where the narrative states the total administration
count; and on \texttt{regimen\_compliance} (16.2\% vs.\ 1.5\% and 2.6\%), where the
substantive templates are windowed counts of the primary component that can often be read
off the same stated dates and counts.  The templated arm, which writes the same fields
with different phrasing, shows the same pattern (46.0\%, 31.8\%, 15.8\%).  We read this as
evidence that the residual narrative advantage is \emph{summary leakage}: the rendering
step pre-computes or foregrounds the answer.  It is not evidence that prose is easier for
the model to reason over than JSON, which is what the previous version claimed.  The
arms that would separate the two explanations (an aggregate-stripped narrative and an
overlay-only compact structured rendering) have not been run.

\label{sec:results:recall}

case the single-chunk index makes retrieval trivial.  For Systems~B and~C,
Table~\ref{tab:recall} reports recall@k pooled over all 13,800 questions with Wilson CIs.
(The previous version reported the mean of per-stratum Wilson CIs, which is why those
intervals were about four times wider than the pooled ones here.  Recall@10 is omitted

System~C retrieves the primary resource more often than System~B at every $k$, and this
advantage \emph{does} appear in end-to-end accuracy once N/A questions are set aside:
on substantive questions C edges B overall ($+0.50$~pp cluster CI [$+0.09$, $+0.91$])
and leads on \texttt{cross\_resource} and \texttt{temporal\_lookup}
(Table~\ref{tab:family_results}).  The aggregate deficit of C relative to B is entirely
an N/A effect, produced by the typed filter concentrating the context on the component
that exists.  A drop-one ablation over C's primitives remains to be run and is the most
informative next experiment on the structured side.

System~B's context is about nine times larger than System~A's on average and its total
cost 4.4 times higher; on a cost-per-correct basis the ratio is about five.  The B mean is
inflated by a heavy tail (maximum 128,385 prompt tokens) that the word-count chunker
could not split (\S\ref{sec:methods:systems}).  System~C's filtering halves B's context
and gives the lowest p50 and p99 latency.  These cost comparisons are between
pipelines that differ in what they put in context, not between representations of
equal size.

\subsection{No-Retrieval Baseline}
\label{sec:results:noretrieval}

System~N passes each question to the answer LLM with empty context and the unchanged
system prompt, which instructs the model to respond ``N/A'' when the answer is not in the
context.  It scores 25.2\% overall: 60.5\% on N/A questions and 0.9\% on substantive
questions.  Between 96\% and 100\% of its correct answers at each tier are N/A-truth
questions.  System~N is therefore not a measure of what the model can guess from the
question text; it is a measure of how often the prompt's abstention instruction happens
to coincide with an N/A ground truth.  The previous version's decomposition of accuracy
into a ``question-text-attributable floor'' and a ``retrieval-attributable lift'' should
be read accordingly: the lift over N on substantive questions is essentially the whole
substantive accuracy (A $+15.1$, B $+14.2$, C $+14.7$~pp, cluster CIs all excluding
zero and overlapping one another), and the lift on N/A questions is the difference
between abstaining by default and abstaining after reading context.  We withdraw the
earlier statement that the systems are ``actively misled by retrieved context'' at
Tier~3; retrieval adds about 13~pp there for every arm.

\subsection{Templated-Narrative Arm}
\label{sec:results:templated}

System~A-T scores 41.3\% against A's 40.6\% (cluster-bootstrap $+0.74$~pp
[$+0.16$, $+1.33$]).  On substantive questions A-T trails A ($-1.19$~pp [$-1.61$,
$-0.75$]) and on N/A questions it leads (79.9\% vs.\ 76.4\%).  Per-family cluster CIs
(A-T minus A): \texttt{temporal\_comparison} $+10.21$ [$+8.71$, $+11.71$];
\texttt{regimen\_compliance} $+1.79$ [$+0.76$, $+2.86$]; \texttt{temporal\_lookup}
$-0.22$ [$-1.12$, $+0.66$]; \texttt{cross\_resource} $-2.25$ [$-4.88$, $+0.38$];
\texttt{regimen\_aggregation} $-7.29$ [$-8.54$, $-6.04$].  The two large effects are
both N/A effects: on \texttt{temporal\_comparison} the templated text yields N/A on
81.7\% of N/A questions against 66.2\% for the LLM text, and on
\texttt{regimen\_aggregation} the LLM prose yields correct counts on 15.8\% of substantive
questions against 6.0\% for the templated text, despite both containing the total count.
We conclude that a deterministic template is at least as good a narrative source as an
LLM for this task, which is useful operationally (no hallucination risk, no generation
cost), but we withdraw the previous claim that a per-family hybrid would beat both ``by
construction'': that holds only under in-sample oracle selection of the family-optimal
style.
